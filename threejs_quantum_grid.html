BB84 on one fiber

Every quantum fiber in the live grid sends its keys as single photons (QKD). A spy has to guess how each photon was prepared, and every wrong guess leaves errors that Alice and Bob can count.

Alice sends photons in random bases; Bob measures in random bases; they keep the rounds where the bases match. A spy who measures and resends must guess the basis.

Grover's search for a key

Grover's search finds one key among N in about √N tries instead of N. For AES-128 that is like halving the key length, which is why new grid equipment should use AES-256.

Breaking RSA-2048 is a different job, for Shor's algorithm. Today's largest machines have about 156 qubits, against fewer than 1 million noisy qubits needed by the latest estimate (Gidney 2025): a gap of about 4 orders of magnitude.

Teleporting a qubit

A future capability, not part of the live grid. If the fibers also shared entangled photon pairs, a qubit could move from the control center to a substation. This tab checks whether today's fiber noise would allow it.

Alice measures two qubits and sends Bob two classical bits over the network; Bob fixes his qubit. Nothing travels faster than the fiber, and the swapping nodes on the way never hold a key. In the live grid, switch to Detailed and turn on Layers → Teleportation view to see where it would work.

Computed here: a 3-qubit state-vector simulation. Each fiber adds independent bit and phase flips at its error rate (our modelling choice: BB84 measures those two rates, not how they correlate); a spy who intercepts and resends adds a bit flip or a phase flip, never both. The hops combine exactly as Pauli channels. 11% is the key-distribution threshold; for teleportation itself the benchmark is beating 2/3 average fidelity, the best a classical channel can do. Notebook 3 in this repo has the same circuit in Qiskit.

The same fiber in four quantum toolkits

When the project runs on its own server, every quantum fiber in the live grid can be re-run as a real quantum program: on Qiskit, NVIDIA CUDA-Q, Quantinuum Guppy or qBraid's cloud simulators.

What each engine does

  • NVIDIA CUDA-Q runs one BB84 round per photon, with the spy copying it into a spare qubit.
  • Quantinuum Guppy lets the spy really measure the photon and send a fresh one.
  • qBraid packs a whole fiber's rounds into one cloud job.

NVIDIA CUDA-Q · one BB84 round

@cudaq.kernel
def bb84_round(bit: int, basis: int, eve_on: int, eve_basis: int, flip: int):
    q = cudaq.qubit()
    anc = cudaq.qubit()
    if bit == 1: x(q)
    if basis == 1: h(q)
    if eve_on == 1:              # Eve measures in her basis:
        if eve_basis == 1: h(q)  # copy into an ancilla nobody reads
        x.ctrl(q, anc)
        if eve_basis == 1: h(q)
    ...
    mz(q)

Next, and not built

Where the lab goes after the hackathon, and the ideas we chose to leave out.

Next

  • Hardware: teleportation between two chips over real fiber (today's run kept all three qubits on one Rigetti chip).
  • GPU: qBraid's GPU simulators to re-check every fiber at once (35-qubit state vector, or tensor networks for larger circuits, and run whole teleportation routes as one circuit).
  • Cost: a sourced cost model; we found no published QKD prices.

Not built

There is no AI assistant, GraphQL API or Next.js app in this project; they were ideas on our technology list (ranked here). Teleportation trips no breaker: it needs a classical message, so it is never faster than the fiber.

Satellites

Not part of the design: the team removed them. Chattanooga's links are all under 23 km, well inside fiber QKD's reach; satellites stay a roadmap idea for statewide distances.
