Quantum keys for Chattanooga's power grid

A working model of the city's real grid whose control links get their keys as single photons (quantum key distribution, QKD). Tap a fiber heavily and the photons give the spy away; a careful tap mostly hides, but only costs key rate. On an alarm the link switches to backup post-quantum encryption and stays up.

    Step 2: The live grid

    Main demo

    The 3D twin of the grid. BB84, the standard recipe for sending a key photon by photon, runs four times a second on every QKD fiber.

    TryPlace a spy on a fiber and watch the network detect it and respond on its own. Then find a route between two sites: it passes through as few sites as possible, because each one re-encrypts the key and could read it.

    Switch between Chattanooga, Tennessee's 500 kV backbone and test networks. The route search takes an error-rate cutoff (11% by default) and falls back to post-quantum keys when no clean quantum route is left.

    Step 3: The photon line

    One fiber, up close

    One fiber in 3D: Alice's bits and bases, a spy in the middle, and Bob's results.

    TrySend photons one at a time, turn the spy on, and watch the error rate climb.

    The error rate is the QBER: the share of key bits that arrive wrong. A spy who measures the photons pushes it up, which is how the spy is caught.

What is real, what is assumed

The map data and the physics are real. Where the fiber runs and which sites hold the keys are our assumptions.

Real

  • Substations, power lines and their lengths, from OpenStreetMap.
  • The physics of BB84, checked against Qiskit, CUDA-Q, Guppy and qBraid.

Assumed

  • Fiber runs along every power line.
  • Which sites hold the keys: the trusted relays, sites that briefly hold a key to pass it on.

Demonstration

  • Grover's search on routes is a demonstration: a classical search already finds the exact route. It shows notebook 2b's circuit working on the grid problem, not a speed-up.
